## How our cutting techniques shape the result

Clippers cut with moving blades. Guards help control how much hair remains, while adjustments and technique create transitions between lengths. Wahl's guide explains that numbered combs represent remaining hair length and that the taper lever provides intermediate adjustments. [Wahl's clipper guide](https://www.wahl.co.uk/content-hub/product-guides/hair-clipper-buying-guide/).

Scissors, also called shears, let us cut sections at chosen angles and work around the shape of the head. They can also shorten and blend hair over a comb. Sam Villa's professional guidance describes scissor-over-comb as a technique with its own tool considerations. [Sam Villa's shear guidance](https://www.samvilla.com/pages/faqs).

Professional technique brings those tools together with purpose. We focus on the intended shape, your hair, and the details that create a polished finish. A scissor cut can be structured and precise; a clipper cut can include a soft blend. The choice begins with the result you want to wear.

## Compare the result before comparing the tools

| Your priority | What to discuss |
| --- | --- |
| Very close sides or a skin fade | Clipper-based work and the desired shortest length |
| Longer hair around the ears | A shape that retains coverage, often using scissors |
| Movement through a medium-length top | Sectioning, layering, and how the hair falls naturally |
| Short sides with a longer top | A combination of techniques may be appropriate |
| Growing out an old fade | Preserve needed length while reshaping selected areas |

Tell us what you liked and what you would change about your previous haircut. Perhaps you want more coverage on the sides or enough front length to sweep over. Those details help us choose a technique and shape around your preferences.

If you specifically prefer a tool for comfort or sensory reasons, that is useful information too. Mention it when confirming the service so the appointment can be planned appropriately.

## Explain length and everyday styling

Bring a photo of your own hair when you liked it, if you have one. It shows us how your hair behaves at a length you enjoyed and gives our conversation a useful visual starting point.

Tell us whether you air-dry, use a dryer, apply product, or prefer a quick comb-through. We can discuss a finish that suits your routine so you leave with a clear idea of how to wear the cut at home.

Before cutting, confirm with us how much will remain at the front, around the ears, and at the crown. Ask us to show the proposed amount with a comb or fingers, especially if keeping length is your priority.

At the end, ask us how to recreate the finish and when to return for professional upkeep. Maintaining the balance and outline in our chair helps keep the result looking considered between visits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are scissor cuts always longer?

No. Scissors can create short shapes, including work close to the head over a comb. However, a request for very close or skin-faded sections usually involves other tools.

### Can one haircut use both scissors and clippers?

Yes. Shorter sides and a longer shaped top are a common reason to combine techniques. Ask about the finished length rather than assuming a single tool must handle the whole cut.

### Will a scissor cut last longer?

Not automatically. Starting length, outline, growth, and how precise you want the result to remain determine when it feels ready for a refresh.
