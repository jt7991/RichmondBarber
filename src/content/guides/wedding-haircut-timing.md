## Use a trial to find your best timing

There is no single haircut date that suits every groom. A sharply defined short cut may look the way you want immediately, while you might prefer a longer style after several washes. Your own experience is more useful than a universal rule.

A practical approach is to get a trial cut several weeks before the wedding, then photograph it on the day and at intervals afterward. Note the day you like the shape best and how easily you can style it yourself. This creates a specific interval to discuss with your barber when booking the final cut.

Wedding barber guidance commonly builds in advance planning: Doorbell Barbers' [wedding grooming checklist](https://home.doorbellbarbers.com/downloads/doorbell-wedding-checklist.pdf) proposes a trial six weeks ahead and a final cut 10–14 days ahead. Treat that as one provider's planning example, not a rule for every hairstyle or a Richmond Barbershop policy.

## Build a schedule around the photographs

Work backward from your first important photos, which may happen before the ceremony. Include travel, a rehearsal, and any earlier celebration where you also want the haircut to look its best.

| Planning stage | Decision to make |
| --- | --- |
| Several weeks ahead | Test any unfamiliar haircut, beard shape, or styling approach. |
| After the trial | Choose how many days of growth you prefer before photos. |
| When reserving final services | Confirm haircut, beard trim, or shave selections and appointment times. |
| Wedding week | Recheck the schedule, travel time, and products you already know how to use. |
| Photo day | Allow time for your established grooming routine and getting dressed. |

For example, if your trial looks best to you five days after the cut, a final appointment around five days before your photos is a reasonable starting point to discuss. It is a personal planning example, not a promise that five days works for everyone.

## Make beard and shave decisions separately

Your beard may need a different schedule from your hair. Decide whether you want a shaped beard, short stubble, or a clean-shaven face before arranging services. A trim preserves length; a shave removes hair close to the skin.

If you normally wear a beard, a wedding-week switch to a clean shave changes your appearance substantially. Try the intended look early enough to decide whether you like it. Likewise, test an unfamiliar shave routine ahead of time so you know how your skin responds before attaching it to a fixed photo deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I get my wedding haircut the day before?

Only if you already know you prefer the result that soon after a cut and the timing works for you. A trial is the best way to choose your own interval.

### When should I book a wedding beard trim?

Use your preferred appearance after a normal trim as the starting point, then discuss the date and photo schedule with the barber. Your haircut and beard trim do not have to follow the same interval.

### Can the whole wedding party come together?

Contact the shop to discuss availability before making group plans. Do not assume a single booking reserves multiple people or a private appointment block.
